# Worked example: Battlecard Review

**Fixture:** `fixtures/nexus-product-marketing/battlecard-taskforge.md` (the Nexus sales battlecard against Taskforge; the fixture brief is in the same folder).
**Rubric:** Battlecard Review (`battlecard-review`), version 1.0, built on Klue's published battlecard guidance, fetched through the ProductRubric MCP server.
**Produced:** Oct 20, 2026, by an AI assistant running the rubric exactly as written. Checked against the fixture's answer key (`expected-findings.md` in the fixture folder).

---

**Verdict: Do not circulate yet**

The card's winning-side material is good, and that is the problem: a card that arms a rep to win and gives them nothing for the deals Nexus loses, while two of its competitor claims are a year and a half old, will be quoted in rooms where it does the rep harm. The missing losing-side half is the gate this rubric sets, and this card does not attempt it.

Evidence available to this review beyond the card itself: the team's competitive analysis (`fixtures/nexus-competitive-analysis/competitive-analysis-2026.md`), which is where this card's stalest claim came from, and which shares its date problem. No live competitor sources were checked for this run.

## 1. Claims about the competitor are sourced and dated: Needs work

Sources exist on this card, which keeps it off the floor, and they are old enough to be dangerous. The pricing snapshot quotes Taskforge at $9 "per their 2024 pricing page" in a document a rep will read as current in October 2026. The Freshness section's one dated claim, "Taskforge has no mobile app (checked March 2025)," is nineteen months old in the section whose name promises the opposite; if Taskforge shipped an app in the meantime, a rep will announce its absence to a buyer holding it. And one claim has no source at all: "their roadmap has been 'coming' since the 2024 acquisition, and development has stalled since," an assertion about a competitor's internal state, placed in objection handling, where a rep is most likely to repeat it verbatim. Fix: re-source every competitor claim to a page checked this quarter, date each one inline, and cut the stalled-development claim or produce its evidence.

## 2. The competitor's strengths are stated honestly: Holds

The overview concedes what a Taskforge rep would claim (price, simplicity, a loyal base, a board people like) and locates Nexus's advantage where those strengths run out (scale, sprawl, the Friday reporting job), rather than pretending the strengths are not real. A rep briefed from this card will not be surprised by Taskforge's best argument, which is what this criterion protects.

## 3. The card arms both directions: wins, losses, and landmines: Missing

The card has a Why We Win section and no Why We Lose section at all: not a thin one, none. The omission is not abstract. The overview itself sets up the losing deal precisely (cheaper, simpler, beloved by teams under 20), and a rep who meets that deal, the most common Taskforge deal there is, has nothing on this card: no honest account of when Taskforge is the right choice, no exit that keeps the relationship, no landmine for the return visit. The landmines that do exist are good (the roadmap-date question is a genuinely sharp one), which makes the one-direction arming a choice rather than an oversight. Fix: write the losing half before this card is quoted again (where Taskforge wins, what to say that is both honest and useful, and what would have to change for the deal to come back).

## 4. Usable in the moment: say it, show it: Needs work

The Say material is strong: the spotting signals are observable in a first call ("docs are in a wiki and the roadmap is a spreadsheet" is something a prospect actually says), and the questions are phrased to be spoken aloud. The Show half is absent: no claim on the card carries a link or a reference a rep can produce (the one-pass migration, the reporting comparison, the pricing totals are all asserted, none shown), so a buyer who asks "can I see that?" ends the rep's best moment. Fix: attach one showable artifact to each Why We Win claim, starting with the migration the messaging already promises.

## 5. Freshness is owned: Needs work

The card carries a last-updated line (October 2026), which is more than most cards manage, and then undercuts it: the Owner field reads "(unassigned)," and the content proves the date is decorative, since a March 2025 check survived an October 2026 update. Freshness on a battlecard is a person's job with a cadence, not a date in a header. Fix: name the owner, set the refresh trigger (a competitor pricing or release event, or quarterly, whichever comes first), and make the update check the claims, not the header.

## Before this goes to the field

1. Write the Why We Lose section. This is the gate: until the card arms both directions, it does not circulate.
2. Re-source and re-date every competitor claim; cut the stalled-development line or evidence it.
3. Replace the 2024 pricing in the snapshot with current pricing, dated in the table where reps will read it.
4. Attach a showable artifact to each Why We Win claim.
5. Name the card's owner and its refresh trigger.
