# Expected findings: Nexus product marketing documents

Answer keys for the four documents in this folder, one per rubric. A competent run should find the planted weaknesses below and judge the rest of each document as sound, ordinary work. None of these is a disaster; a fair overall verdict for each is in the middle range of its rubric's forms.

## positioning.md (Positioning Review)

1. **Value asserted, proof anecdotal.** "Customers tell us Nexus cuts status meetings in half" is the whole proof for the lead value claim. A halving is a number, and a number needs a source; as written, the proof cell repeats the claim in a friendlier voice.
2. **The target market is two sizes at once.** "Teams of 2 to 2,000, in any industry" sits in the same document as attributes built for software teams shipping weekly, and the document's own best-fit line ("software product teams of 20 to 200") contradicts the wide claim. Positioning that fits everyone positions for no one.
3. **Alternatives that dodge the real contest.** The competitive alternatives are spreadsheets, to-do apps, and a legacy suite, never the direct project platforms a buyer actually cross-shops, so the claimed category ("project platform") is never earned against the tools that define it.
4. **A table-stakes attribute listed as unique.** "Kanban boards with drag and drop" is in the Unique Attributes list. It is neither unique nor, in Dunford's terms, an attribute the value story uses.

## messaging-sprints-2.md (Messaging Review)

1. **The pitch opens with the product, not the insight.** The first paragraph is a feature inventory (rollover, metrics, trends, a view) before any problem exists on the page. The sales pitch discipline this document follows elsewhere (its story section knows the lost hour matters) is inverted where it counts most: the opening.
2. **The brand is the hero of the story.** In The Story, Nexus "arrives," acts, and "hands her the hour back, every week, forever," while the team lead only suffers and receives. The customer should be the hero and the product the guide; the closing line ("The team just ships") gestures at it after the damage is done.
3. **An unverifiable proof quote.** "It changed how our whole company works," attributed to a role at an unnamed Fortune 500 company, cannot be checked, credited, or used in front of a careful buyer. The Acme proof point below it shows the expected shape (a named company, a countable result).
4. **The hardest objection answered with a shrug.** "We already live in another tool" gets "Switching is easy. Most teams are up and running in a day," with no import path, no timeline, no help offered, in a document whose own battlecard (a fixture in this folder) promises a one-pass import it never mentions here.
5. **No real ask.** The pitch closes on "Try Nexus today": no trial, no next step, no named action a listener can take. After eight steps of build, the close asks for nothing in particular.

## launch-plan-sprints-2.md (Launch Plan Review)

1. **A Tier 1 label on Tier 3 resourcing.** The plan calls the launch a market moment, then resources it with one email, one blog post, a changelog post, and a homepage pill: no PR, no webinar, no customer story, no partner push. The tier is a claim the asset list refutes.
2. **Enablement after the launch it enables.** Sales and support are enabled on November 20, two days after launch day, so the first and busiest 48 hours of questions land on teams who have not had the session. (The plan even expects support questions in week one.)
3. **An audience with a message and no asset.** Integration partners get a message ("Cycle events are now available in the API") and "Asset: TBD," with dev relations owning a deliverable that does not exist yet, on a plan whose launch assets are due final two days before launch.
4. **Success measures without instrumentation.** "Sprint feature adoption rises 25%" names no baseline (25% above what, measured where) and "launch email click rate above the lifecycle average" names no average. Two of the three measures cannot be scored at the December 2 retro as written.

## battlecard-taskforge.md (Battlecard Review)

1. **A stale, unsourced competitor claim in the section named Freshness.** "Taskforge has no mobile app (checked March 2025)" is nineteen months old on a card dated October 2026, with no source. If it has gone false since, a rep will say it in front of a buyer holding the app.
2. **No Why We Lose section.** The card arms a rep to win and gives them nothing for the deals Nexus loses, which the competitor overview itself sets up: Taskforge is cheaper and simpler, and teams under 20 people love it. A rep meets exactly that deal with no honest answer prepared.
3. **Stale pricing presented in a snapshot.** The pricing table quotes Taskforge's $9 from "their 2024 pricing page" in a document a rep will read as current. The sourcing is honest in the small print and misleading in the table, which is where it will be used.
4. **FUD without evidence.** "Their roadmap has been 'coming' since the 2024 acquisition, and development has stalled since" asserts a competitor's internal state with no source at all, in objection handling, the place a rep is most likely to repeat it verbatim.
5. A smaller note: the card's Owner field is unassigned, on a document whose failure mode is going stale. Whoever owns freshness owns the card; here, no one does.
