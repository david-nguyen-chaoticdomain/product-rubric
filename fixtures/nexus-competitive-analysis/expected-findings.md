# Expected findings: Nexus competitive analysis

The answer key for the Competitive Analysis Review rubric, for `competitive-analysis-2026.md`. A competent run should find the planted weaknesses below and judge the rest as the sound work it is: the competitor portraits are fair, the status quo is included, and strengths are conceded honestly. A fair reliance verdict is the middle form (rely on it with the noted gaps), not the bottom one.

1. **The competitor set is cherry-picked.** The Purpose names Workstack as "growing quickly in our segment" and then the analysis never mentions it again: no portrait, no strengths, no data. An analysis that omits the entrant it itself flags as the fast mover cannot support a planning decision, however good its two portraits are.
2. **Stale evidence presented as current.** The Taskforge portrait rests on "their pricing page (March 2025)" for both the $9 price and the claim that there is no mobile app, in a document dated October 2026. Nineteen months is several pricing cycles in this market; either claim may have gone false, and the document gives the reader no fresher check. (The same stale mobile claim appears in the team's battlecard, which is how stale analysis propagates into the field.)
3. **Vendor marketing repeated as evidence.** Planora's "99.99% uptime and more than 10,000 teams" is their homepage copy, carried into the analysis with no verification, no source discipline, and no distinction from the findings the team gathered itself. The portrait's one piece of gathered evidence (two interviews for the weak daily surface) is also thin, though at least its size is disclosed.
4. **The headline recommendation argues against the analysis.** "Where the Open Ground Is" sends the 2027 growth motion at teams under 20, the segment where the analysis itself says Taskforge's price and simplicity are strongest and where Nexus's differentiators (sprints, roadmap, unified workspace) matter least, and the closing section then holds both that recommendation and its opposite ("win the over-20 segment on the unified workspace") without noticing the conflict. The evidence gathered supports the second; the recommendation asserts the first.
5. **An unsourced market.** "A $4.2B market growing at 18% a year" opens the analysis with no source, no definition of the market it measures, and no date. Everything downstream (the size of the prize, the growth story told to planning) inherits a number nobody can check.
