# Expected findings: Invite flow findings report

The answer key for the Findings Review rubric, for `findings-invite-flow.md`. The report's bones are good: findings carry counts, participants are quoted by ID, the method is stated, and the limits note is honest. The planted weaknesses are the failures a claim audit exists to catch, and two of them are provable from the document itself, against its own appendix and its own summary.

1. **Finding 1 overstates its own evidence.** "Admins cannot tell whether their invitation was sent" is a universal claim; the finding's own text says two of five hesitated and all five found the status within a minute. The evidence supports a hesitation at the point of sending, not an inability, and the difference decides the fix (a plainer confirmation, not a redesign). Claim audit: partly supported, stated as fully supported.
2. **Finding 6 has no evidence and contradicts Finding 2 and the Summary.** "Participants distrusted the review step" appears with no quote, no count, and no observation, and it inverts Finding 2's evidenced account (the review step caught two real errors, and a participant praised it) and the Summary's own line ("participants trusted the review step to catch mistakes"). Claim audit: unsupported.
3. **Finding 3 is contradicted by the report's appendix.** "Nobody starts from the Members tab" is disproved by the appendix's Task 1 note: three of five started from the Members tab. This also bears on the study plan's Hypothesis 1, which the data actually supports and the finding reports backwards. Claim audit: unsupported (contradicted by the report's own record).
4. **Finding 4 is a recommendation wearing a finding's number.** "The invite button should be moved into the project header" is a design prescription. No participant evidence is attached, and given the appendix (most participants started at the Members tab), the underlying preference it asserts is doubtful as well. It belongs in Recommendations, argued for, not in Findings, counted.
5. **The Summary inflates severity.** "Three critical blockers that must be fixed before it ships" is not earned by the body: Finding 1's own evidence is a one-minute hesitation, Finding 5 builds a "critical barrier" on one participant finding the role names "a bit formal," and no other finding claims blocker weight. A reader who only reads the Summary (most readers) takes away a severity the evidence never supports. Claim audit for Finding 5's "critical barrier": unsupported as graded.
